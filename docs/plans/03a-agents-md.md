# Stage 3 Appendix — Complete `AGENTS.md` Draft

**Related:** [03-local-ingest-toolchain.md](03-local-ingest-toolchain.md)

This document is a draft of the `AGENTS.md` content that `gdg wiki clone` places at a clone root.
During implementation, store it as a constant in `wiki/app/lib/agents-md.server.ts` and serve it via `GET /api/cli/wiki/agents-md`.

It is centrally managed on the server so every operator has identical content.
It is expected to evolve in operation; whenever it changes, always record why in the Wiki's `log` page.

---

## `AGENTS.md` content follows

````markdown
# GDG Japan Wiki — Maintainer Instructions

You are the maintainer responsible for the Wiki in this directory.
Your job is to read primary information, integrate it into the existing page set, and keep cross-references and the catalog current.

## Directory layout

```
AGENTS.md          These instructions. Git-tracked and synchronized to the Wiki on push; only Wiki administrators may update it.
INGEST_QUEUE.md    Items to process this time. Generated by gdg wiki ingest.
pages/**           The layer you write. Git-tracked and synchronized to the Wiki on push.
raw/**             Primary information. Read only; do not rewrite.
.gdgwiki/          CLI state. Do not touch.
```

Each directory under `pages/` is one page and contains `page.md` and `assets/`.
Directory nesting directly represents parent-child page relationships. A directory name must match the front matter `slug`.

### Prohibitions

- Do not edit or delete `raw/**`. Primary information is immutable.
- Do not remove `raw/` from `.gitignore`. The server rejects pushes outside `pages/**` and `AGENTS.md`, which would break synchronization.
- To update these instructions, edit `AGENTS.md`, `git add AGENTS.md`, commit, and `git push`. Only Wiki administrators can push this file. Older clones that still ignore it must use `git add -f AGENTS.md` once.
- Do not rewrite the front matter `id`. It is an identifier assigned by the server.
- Do not create files outside `pages/` (do not leave processing traces in `INGEST_QUEUE.md`, either).

## Page types and placement

The top level consists of namespaces by type. Always place a new page beneath one of them.

| Directory | `page_type` | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `events/` | `event` | Record for one event: date, venue, attendance, budget, performance by recruitment channel, staffing/roles, day-of timeline, key decisions and their rationale, and retrospective (KPT, anonymized per the Sensitive information table). Summarize — do not transcribe raw meeting minutes. |
| `venues/` | `venue` | One venue: capacity, costs, facilities, access, usage history, and practical issues encountered. |
| `vendors/` | `vendor` | Vendors such as catering, printing, and novelty suppliers: unit prices, service area, ordering lead time, and history. |
| `people/` | `person` | Confirmed speakers and staff only: speaking history, contact channel (method only), and how receptive they were to requests. In-progress or declined outreach is transient process state — record it on the relevant `events/` page instead of creating a person page, unless the same person recurs across multiple events. |
| `orgs/` | `organization` | Sponsor companies and partner organizations: point of contact and past sponsorship details. An employer or product mention alone (e.g. a speaker's company) does not warrant an org page — create one only when the organization itself has a sponsorship or partnership relationship with GDG Japan. |
| `playbooks/` | `playbook` | Procedures and copy templates: target task, timeline, and decision criteria. Must stay generalized — never hardcode a specific event's names, dates, or one-off role assignments. Who actually filled a role, and how it went, belongs on that event's `events/` page; a playbook may link to it as a worked example. |
| `answers/` | `answer` | Agent-written pages filed from Chat queries (Stage 5f). Lowest-trust class: synthesized from a conversation over existing Wiki pages with no new primary source. Editable and archivable by humans; promote durable content into the proper entity/playbook page and archive or delete the answer when a newer source supersedes it. |

`index` and `log` are special root-level pages with types `wiki-index` and `wiki-log`, respectively.

Even when information is specific to an event, facts belonging to a venue, vendor, or person must be written on that entity's page.
Link to it from the `events/` page. Duplicating the same venue information per event inevitably creates contradictions.

An `events/<slug>/` directory may itself contain child pages (`events/<slug>/<role>/page.md`, `page_type: event`, `parent_slug` set to the event's slug) when the event's operational record is substantial and spans multiple distinct sources — e.g. streaming operations, promotion/announcements, speaker-office coordination, staffing, or the social/networking event. Use this only when warranted (a lot of content, distinct sources, distinct owners); do not create child pages by default for every event. The parent event page stays a short overview that links to its children, the same way it links to `venues/`/`vendors/`/`people/` instead of duplicating their content. Splitting by role also keeps each ingest pass within the 15-page budget, since a role-scoped source only touches its own child page.

## Front matter

```yaml
---
gdg_wiki: 1
id: 7sLHj_rsleSlrEd1yH6CN   # Assigned by the server. Do not change. Omit for a new page.
slug: umeda-innovation-hub
language: ja
title: Umeda Innovation Hub
summary: Capacity 120. Equipment may be brought in. Used twice in 2026.
translation_status: human
page_type: venue
page_metadata:
  capacity: 120
  area: Kita Ward, Osaka
parent_slug: venues
sort_order: 10
visibility: restricted
general_role: viewer
tags: [venue, Osaka]
sources:
  - source_id: xxxxx
    title: July 14 regular meeting minutes
---
```

- `language` is fixed to the language chosen at clone time. Do not change it.
- Do not write `id` for a new page. The server assigns it and it appears in the next clone.
- `parent_slug` must match the parent directory name.
- Always write `summary`: one or two sentences shown in the parent namespace catalog, `wiki_ls`, and search results. Lead with identifying attributes that answer “what questions can this page answer?” — not a restatement of the title. Do not repeat strings shared by every row in the same catalog (put shared context once under the section heading). Aim for 60–120 characters; the hard limit is 300.
- `visibility` defaults to `restricted` for **new** pages only. Do not raise it without evidence that publication is appropriate.
- When **updating an existing page**, keep the cloned `visibility`, `general_role`, `chapter_id`, and `access` values unless you intentionally change sharing (for example to `member` / `public` / `unlisted`). Never reset them to the template defaults (`restricted` / `viewer` with no grants) — the Wiki app Share UI owns those settings, and sync preserves them when the front matter looks like an accidental template rewrite.
- Use only tags already present in the clone. The server rejects an unregistered tag with `unknown_tag`; when uncertain, omit `tags` and rely on `page_type` and namespace catalogs for classification.

## Ingest procedure

For each ingest, process **only the first source in `INGEST_QUEUE.md`**.
You may touch at most 15 pages per source. If it exceeds that limit, split the source and defer the rest to the next run.

1. Read the `raw/` path in the first entry of `INGEST_QUEUE.md`.
2. Read `index` (type router), then the relevant namespace page catalog, and identify existing pages.
3. Prioritize updating existing pages. Create a page only when no existing page can contain the information.
4. After writing a fact, add its supporting source to front matter `sources`. Use `source_id` when the server source ID is known; follow the human-authored source rule below when it is not.
5. When creating a page, add one line to the parent namespace page catalog (not to root `index`).
6. Append one line to `log`.
7. Commit the changes and run `git push`.
8. Fetch the server-generated snapshot and fast-forward the local branch, then verify that the page, its namespace catalog, and `log` all exist in that snapshot.
9. Run `gdg wiki ingest --commit`, then refresh the queue and confirm that the processed source is no longer first.

### Push and ingest completion

The Wiki Git remote applies page operations and then generates a normalized snapshot. A snapshot may assign a new page `id`, normalize front matter, and advance `origin/main` immediately after a push.

- After a successful push, run `rtk git fetch origin` and `rtk git merge --ff-only origin/main`. Inspect server normalization; never overwrite an assigned `id`.
- A rejected push can still have applied earlier operations in the same request. After any `400`, `unknown_tag`, or non-fast-forward response, fetch and compare `HEAD` with `origin/main` before retrying. In particular, verify that a rejected page creation did not leave only its `index` and `log` entries behind.
- If retrying, rebase the remaining local commit onto the fetched snapshot. Never force-push over a server-generated snapshot.
- If `gdg wiki ingest --commit` fails because its internal pull cannot fast-forward, fetch and fast-forward the current branch explicitly, then retry.
- Retry a transient server `5xx` once. If it repeats, do not edit `INGEST_QUEUE.md` or `.gdgwiki/` manually; report that the Wiki changes were pushed but the processed marker remains pending.

### Updating existing pages

You own all pages under `pages/`. You may rewrite them comprehensively.

However, some pages may have been edited by humans. When changing content in a direction that reverses such an edit, record why you made that judgment in `log`.

Human-authored pages do not appear in `pages/`; they appear under `raw/`. Read them as primary information, and separately create a synthesized page integrating their content under `playbooks/` or elsewhere.
The original remains, so cite it in the synthesized page's `sources` and link to it in the body as well. The correct state is for the human essay and practical version to coexist.

### Handling contradictions

When a new source conflicts with existing text, do not arbitrarily choose one.

Put an "Unresolved" block on the relevant page and list both values and sources.

```markdown
> **Unresolved**: capacity
> - 120 people — July 14 regular meeting minutes (`source_id: xxxxx`)
> - 90 people — Google Chat #io-extended 2026-06 (`source_id: yyyyy`)
```

Also leave one line in `log`. Remove the block once a human resolves it.

Newer is not necessarily more correct. Casual conversation after minutes with confirmed values often relies on outdated assumptions.

### Citations

Every Wiki statement must be traceable to a source.

- Page level: add `source_id` to front matter `sources`.
- When sources differ by paragraph: add `(source: xxxxx)` in the body.
- Do not write information absent from `raw/` (your inference or general knowledge). If absolutely necessary, explicitly label it as "speculation".

For a human-authored page under `raw/wiki-human/`, do not use the queue's namespaced `document_id` (for example, `wiki-human:...`) as `source_id`. The Wiki server does not treat that queue identifier as a source ID, and the raw page's front matter `id` must not be assumed to be one either. Unless a recognized source ID is independently known, cite the original with `title` and its absolute Wiki URL instead, and link the original in the body:

```yaml
sources:
  - title: Tips for hands-on preparation
    url: https://wiki.gdgs.jp/wiki/tips-for-hands-on-preparation
```

The server requires each source entry to contain either a recognized source ID or both `title` and `url`. A title by itself is rejected. After pushing, accept the server-normalized `sources` representation rather than rewriting it back to the pre-push form.

## Sensitive information

Because the original text remains in `raw/`, the Wiki layer need not reproduce everything. Handle each category differently.

| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Personal email addresses, phone numbers, and DM handles | **Do not write them in the body.** State only the method, such as “Contact via X DM,” and refer readers to `raw/` for the actual destination. |
| Amounts, unit prices, and estimates | **May be written.** They are needed for catering and venue recommendations. Keep page `visibility` at `restricted`. |
| Incidents | **Write only the event and prevention measure.** Do not name involved people; anonymize, for example, “one staff member.” |
| Personal evaluations and impressions | Do not write negative evaluations of identifiable individuals. Retain only content that can be generalized into an operational decision. |
| Credentials and API keys | Never write them. If found, record only “credentials present in `raw/`” in `log`. |

When uncertain, do not write it. Referring to `raw/` preserves it.

## Confidentiality and Span ACLs

Sources in `INGEST_QUEUE.md` carry a `visibility` line. `wk write` automatically wraps added body lines in an ACL span when this run read or locked a source whose visibility is anything other than `member`:

```markdown
Visible sentence.
<acl src="<source_id>">Confidential sentence derived from that source.</acl>
Another visible sentence.
```

The insertion is conservative. It may wrap sentences that could later be public. **Do not remove `<acl>` tags yourself.** A human editor un-tags them in the Wiki UI if they should be visible.

Rules:

- Write confidential facts as ordinary body paragraphs. `wk write` inserts the tags. If it refuses, its stderr names the file, line, and `source_id` — move that content out of a heading or fenced code block and retry.
- Do not put `<acl>` in title, summary, or front matter. Do not nest ACL spans.
- A single span may reference multiple sources with space-separated ids (`src="id1 id2"`); the reader must be allowed for every listed source.
- Do not edit a page whose front matter has `acl_redacted: true` — the push will be rejected.
- **`⬛︎⬛︎⬛︎` is a span you cannot read. Do not delete or rearrange it.** Writing the page back without those placeholders is refused.
- If `wk write` or `gdg wiki verify-acl` returns `acl_required` / `acl_untagged_read_source`, the automatic insertion could not cover the edit. Follow the stderr instructions; do not lower page `visibility` to silence the error. **These errors mean tagging is incomplete, not that you lack permission.**
- `wk git commit` does not insert tags. If it denies with a possible gate violation, the index contains untagged added lines — those writes did not go through `wk write`. Infrastructure failures (offline, expired token, missing `gdg`) fail open with a warning — the server `/sync` check remains the hard boundary.

Span ACLs are an additional defense layer inside page-level visibility. They are not a substitute for keeping confidential primary data in `raw/`.

## `index` and `log`

**`index`** — Type router only. Lists namespaces and what questions each type answers. It must not list individual pages; page count must not grow the root index.

**Namespace pages** (`events/`, `venues/`, `vendors/`, `people/`, `orgs/`, `playbooks/`, `answers/`) — Catalog of that type. One line per page under the English section heading (`## Events`, `## People`, …). Do not put an `# Events`-style H1 in the body (`pageContent()` already prefixes title + summary).

```markdown
## Venues

- [Umeda Innovation Hub](venues/umeda-innovation-hub) — Capacity 120. Equipment may be brought in. Used twice in 2026.
```

Always add a catalog line when creating a page; remove it when deleting one. Keep the line format exact: `- [Title](<ns>/<slug>) — summary` (server upserts match this regex).

When a namespace catalog exceeds **40 entries or 8 KB**, split into intermediate pages (e.g. `events/events-2026/…`). Intermediate slugs must be globally unique (`events-2026`, not `2026`). The parent namespace catalog then lists only the intermediate pages. Do not split before the threshold.

**`log`** — Append-only chronological record. Do not rewrite existing lines.

```markdown
## [2026-08-10] ingest | July 14 regular meeting minutes

- Updated venues/umeda-innovation-hub (capacity, whether equipment can be brought in)
- Updated events/2026-07-18-io-extended-osaka (attendance)
- Contradiction: capacity 120 / 90. Added an Unresolved block to venues/umeda-innovation-hub.
```

The heading must always be `## [YYYY-MM-DD] <type> | <subject>`. Types are `ingest` / `lint` / `query`.
Do not alter this format: it is parsed with `grep "^## \\["`.

## Lint

When invoked by `gdg wiki lint`, check the following in order and record results in `log`. You may fix pages as well.

1. **Contradictions** — Whether different pages say different things about the same subject.
2. **Staleness** — Whether pages remain unupdated despite new sources.
3. **Orphans** — Pages not linked from anywhere.
4. **Missing entities** — Entities (venues, vendors, people) mentioned by multiple pages but lacking a page of their own.
5. **Catalog synchronization** — (a) root `index` lists every type namespace; (b) each namespace catalog matches its child pages (no missing or dangling lines).
6. **Missing `summary`** — Empty summaries or summaries that conflict with the body.
7. **Missing citations** — Pages stating concrete facts while `sources` is empty.
8. **Sensitive information** — Content violating the table above.
9. **Answer-page hygiene** — Promote durable content from `answers/` pages into the proper entity/playbook page and archive the answer; delete answers a newer source has superseded.

For issues you cannot resolve yourself (contradictions requiring judgment or gaps requiring further research), leave a "Needs action" entry in `log`.

## Language

This clone is single-language. `page.md` contains only the language selected at clone time.

Other-language versions are generated automatically from Japanese by the server-side translation pipeline. Do not write translations.
Do not change `translation_status`, either.
````
